LG Dishwasher IE Error Code: What It Means and How to Fix It

Usually an easy fixAbout 15 minutesNot the same as E1
Short answer: IE is a water inlet error. The dishwasher expected the tub to fill but the water level stayed too low. LG lists the usual causes as the house water being off, a closed supply valve, a kinked or crushed inlet hose, a frozen hose, or low water pressure. You may also notice it spraying and draining over and over, or hear an alarm.

Fix it yourself: five checks, in order

  1. Read the code carefully. Make sure the display says IE and not E1. LG treats them as different problems.
  2. Check the house water. Turn on the kitchen or bathroom tap. If nothing comes out, the whole house supply is off and the dishwasher will work again once it returns.
  3. Open the supply valve. Look in the cabinet under the sink and turn the dishwasher supply valve counterclockwise to open it. If water still does not flow, check the valve on the pipe above it the same way. Valves are often left closed after a new installation, a move, or sink repairs.
  4. Check the inlet hose. Follow the hose from the valve to the dishwasher. Straighten any kinks or bends, and move anything heavy that is pressing on it. Replace the hose if it is torn.
  5. Thaw a frozen hose. If the dishwasher is in a cold space, the water in the hose can freeze. Wrap the hose in a towel soaked in warm water until it thaws.

Then start a new cycle and listen for water filling the tub.

When to call a technician

If the water is on, the valves are open and the hose is clear but IE keeps coming back, the cause may be low water pressure in the home, a clogged filter inside the inlet valve, or a faulty inlet valve. If the code appeared right after installation, contact the installer first. Otherwise request service from LG.
