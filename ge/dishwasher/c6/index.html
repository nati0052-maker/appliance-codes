GE Dishwasher C6 Error Code: What It Means and How to Fix It

Usually an easy fixAbout 10 minutesMeans water too cool
Short answer: C6 means the water coming into the dishwasher is not hot enough. GE says incoming water should be at least 120 degrees F. The fix is usually on the supply side: the water heater setting, cold water sitting in the pipes, or other hot water use in the house at the same time.

Fix it yourself: five checks, in order

  1. Run the kitchen faucet hot first. Before you start the dishwasher, run the hot water at the sink next to it until it comes out hot. This pushes the cold water out of the line so the dishwasher fills with hot water.
  2. Check the water temperature. If you have a kitchen thermometer, hold it in a glass of hot water from that faucet. GE says the water reaching the dishwasher should be at least 120 degrees F.
  3. Adjust the water heater. If the water is below 120 degrees F, turn the water heater setting up slightly and give it time to recover. Follow the water heater maker's instructions, and do not set it so hot that it becomes a scald risk at the tap.
  4. Avoid other hot water use during the cycle. Do not run the washing machine or take a shower while the dishwasher is running. Both pull hot water away from the dishwasher.
  5. Choose a longer or hotter option. GE suggests using cycle options that make the dishwasher run longer, which gives it more time to heat the water. Then run the cycle again.

If the code appeared once after a cold night or a busy morning of showers, it may not return once the water heater has caught up.

When to call a technician

If the water at the faucet is at least 120 degrees F, you have run it hot before starting, and C6 still appears, the problem may be inside the dishwasher. A related code, C7, means the water temperature sensor circuit is not working. In either case, turn the power off at the breaker for about 30 seconds as a reset, and if the code returns, contact service.
