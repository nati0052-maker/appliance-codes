Samsung Washer 4C Error Code: What It Means and How to Fix It

Usually an easy fixAbout 15 minutesAlso shown as 4E
Short answer: 4C means the washer is not getting enough water during the fill. In most homes the cause is a closed tap, a kinked or frozen hose, or a clogged mesh filter where the hose connects to the washer. These checks need nothing more than pliers.

Fix it yourself: six checks, in order

  1. Open both water taps fully. The taps behind the washer are often half closed after moving or plumbing work.
  2. Check the hoses for kinks. Pushing the washer back against the wall commonly folds the hose. Straighten it and leave a few centimetres of slack.
  3. Check for freezing. In a cold garage or utility room, a frozen hose blocks flow completely. Let it thaw before running a cycle.
  4. Clean the inlet mesh filters. Turn the taps off, unscrew the hoses from the back of the washer, and pull out the small mesh screens with pliers. Rinse out grit and limescale, refit, and reconnect. This is the most common fix in hard-water areas.
  5. Test your water pressure. Run the nearest sink tap. If flow is weak there too, the problem is the home supply, not the washer.
  6. Check the detergent drawer. A drawer packed with hardened detergent can restrict the water path on some models. Remove it and rinse it in warm water.

After the checks, unplug the washer for one minute, plug it back in, and start a short rinse cycle.

When to call a technician

If water reaches the washer at good pressure, the filters are clean, and 4C still appears, the likely fault is the water inlet valve or, less often, the water level sensor. Both need the back or top panel removed. If the washer is under warranty, contact Samsung service before opening it.
