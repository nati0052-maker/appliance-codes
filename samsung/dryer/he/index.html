Samsung Dryer HE Error Code: What It Means and How to Fix It

Some fixes need toolsAbout 30 minutesAlso shown as hE, HC, HC4, 1 HC
Short answer: HE is a heating error. Samsung says it most often means the dryer is getting too hot because hot air cannot escape, and the usual cause is a clogged lint screen or vent. On electric dryers, low supply voltage can also stop the heater working properly. On heat pump dryers, Samsung describes HE and HC as a compressor overheating or heating check.

Fix it yourself: five checks, in order

  1. Clean the lint screen. Pull it out, remove all lint, and rinse it if it looks coated with fabric softener residue. Let it dry fully before refitting. Also clear any lint in the slot it sits in.
  2. Check the vent hose behind the dryer. Unplug the dryer (and turn off the gas valve on a gas model), pull it out, and look for a crushed, kinked or lint-packed hose. Disconnect it and clear any lint you can reach. A screwdriver or nut driver may be needed for the clamp.
  3. Check the outside vent hood. With the dryer running, you should feel strong airflow at the outside vent and the flap should open. Weak airflow points to a blockage somewhere in the duct.
  4. Check the breakers. Electric dryers use a double breaker. If one half has tripped, the dryer can run but heat poorly. Reset any tripped breaker.
  5. Reset the dryer. Unplug it for at least 30 seconds, plug it back in, and run a short timed dry cycle to see whether the code returns.

On a heat pump or condenser dryer there is no outside vent. Clean the lint filter and the heat exchanger filter as your manual describes instead.

When to call a technician

If the lint screen and vent are clear and HE keeps coming back, call for help. On electric dryers, Samsung advises having an electrician check the supply voltage, and checking the wiring at the heating element is a job for a qualified person. Otherwise the cause may be a heating part or sensor inside the dryer, which needs Samsung service or a technician. Do not keep running a dryer that is overheating.
